🤝 The Layman Analogy: The Land Registry

Think of GoDaddy as the City's Land Registry Office. You visit them to buy a "Street Name" (Domain). Once you have the name, you decide which "Map Maker" (Nameserver) will tell people where your house is. Locking your domain is like putting a physical padlock on your property deed so nobody can steal it while you're sleeping.

1. Domain Lifecycle

  • Register: Claiming the land (1-10 years).
  • Renew: Paying your taxes to keep the land.
  • Expire: The "Grace Period" where utilities are cut but you can still pay.
  • Transfer: Moving your deed from one registry to another (e.g., GoDaddy to Namecheap).
⚠️ DEVOPS WARNING: Always enable "Transfer Lock" and "WHOIS Privacy" to prevent social engineering attacks.

2. The "Glue Record" Mystery

Glue Records are used when your domain’s map-maker (Nameserver) is hosted ON the domain itself.

Pro Example

"My nameserver is ns1.example.com. But wait! To find example.com, I need to ask ns1.example.com. That's a circular trap!"

Solution: The Glue Record provides the IP address of the nameserver directly at the Registry.

Step-by-Step DNS Resolution

1

Recursive Resolver (ISP)

"Hey, does anyone know where google.com is?"

2

Root Servers (.)

"I don't know, but here is the address for the .com office."

3

Authoritative Server (GoDaddy/Cloudflare)

"I own this domain! Here is the IP Address you need: 142.250.x.x"

A / AAAA The Address

Connects your domain name to the physical IP address of your server.

example.com. IN A 1.2.3.4
CNAME The Alias

Maps one domain name to another. Used for load balancers or CDNs.

www.example.com. IN CNAME example.com.
SRV Service Discovery

Specifies the location of a specific service (like SIP or LDAP) by port.

_sip._tcp.example.com. 86400 IN SRV 10 60 5060 srv.example.com.
CAA Security Control

Tells the world: "Only Let's Encrypt can issue SSL for me!"

example.com. IN CAA 0 issue "letsencrypt.org"

The Email Notary Protocol

In production, 80% of email delivery issues are caused by missing these 3 TXT records.

1. SPF (The Approved List)

Scenario: "Only my Google Workspace and my local office IP are allowed to send mail."

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ip4:203.0.113.1 ~all
2. DKIM (The Digital Seal)

Scenario: "I'm signing every letter with a wax seal that only my DNS public key can verify."

v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBgkqh...
3. DMARC (The Instruction)

Scenario: "If someone sends a mail claiming to be me but fails SPF/DKIM, throw it in the trash!"

v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:admin@example.com
